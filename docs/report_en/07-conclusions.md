## 7. Conclusions and Future Work

### 7.1 Summary of Core Conclusions

1. **Detection difficulty varies by noise type, and the ranking is stable**: garbled/template/duplicate are easiest to detect (AUC > 0.98), keyword/near-duplicate are hardest (AUC 0.57-0.76); this ranking holds across both the 10% and 5% noise ratios.
2. **Cross-type transfer loses substantial ground (~17-20 points), cross-ratio transfer is nearly lossless**: a detector needs separate calibration per noise type, but not per noise ratio.
3. **AUC overstates cleaning usefulness**: duplicate has AUC as high as 0.986 but P@10% lift < 1 — choosing a cleaning method requires looking at precision under a realistic budget, not just AUC.
4. **The direction-reversal trap is a real methodological pitfall**: "hyper-typical/memorized" noise like template needs a signed prior rule (not generic outlier detection) to be caught, and this same pitfall reappears along the early-detection time axis.
5. **Two types' high AUC is not what it appears**: over 90% of the detection signal for duplicate and unrelated comes from a static text-similarity feature, not training dynamics — these two types cannot be cited as evidence that the "training-dynamics detection" methodology works. The single-feature leave-one-out ablation confirms this independently: `text_nn_sim` is the only metric irreplaceable on both routes (dropping it costs duplicate/unrelated/mixed 0.056-0.092), while dropping any of the other 19 trajectory features individually keeps RF's |Δ| below 0.01 across the board (Section 6.11.3).

6. **On the label-free route, "more features is better" is false**: 43 of 152 IF cells see AUC *rise* after dropping one metric (RF: zero), and the same `converge_epoch` gains 5.2 points when dropped on template but loses 2.1 on near_duplicate. Without labels every dimension enters the outlier distance indiscriminately, so dimensions irrelevant to the current noise type purely dilute the signal — and which ones are irrelevant depends on the very noise type that is unknown. This independently justifies `pooled` taking a max rather than a mean (Section 6.11.3).
7. **Real downstream harm is limited but present**: the spread across the 7 general benchmarks is small, but template at ratio10 is genuinely the worst-performing dataset downstream — the only type where "easy to detect" and "actually harmful" both hold.
8. **Label-free closed-loop cleaning works, but only when the noise is genuinely harmful *and* the scorer points the right way**: on garbled, targeted removal reached 5.7x random precision yet produced no downstream gain (garbled is nearly harmless to begin with); switching to template, which is genuinely harmful, `memo_signed` cleaning recovered 70.3% of the 8.95-point GSM8K gap and 74.4% of the 7-benchmark mean gap — the only positive downstream cleaning gain in the whole report.

9. **When the scorer direction is wrong, cleaning is worse than not cleaning and worse than random removal**: on template, `iforest` (wrong direction) reached only 4.04% targeted precision, less than half of random removal's 9.24%, and after retraining GSM8K fell to 0.4193 — 4.09 points below the uncleaned baseline. The full chain reads "right direction 0.5231 > no cleaning 0.4602 > random removal 0.4526 > wrong direction 0.4193," i.e. cleaning in the wrong direction does active negative work (Section 6.13.2).

10. **On the label-free route 3 metrics beat 20, on all 8 datasets**: narrowing what IsolationForest is fed from 20 features to a greedily-chosen 3 raises AUC by 0.133 on average — template from 0.537 to 0.875, duplicate from 0.528 to 0.744. The current production configuration systematically handicaps itself, and part of what looks like "direction reversal" is really **dimensional dilution**: irrelevant dimensions flatten the outlier distance until the two or three carrying signal are drowned out (Section 6.11.4). But *which* 3 depends on the noise type, and not knowing the type is the premise of the label-free setting.

11. **On mixed noise, the scorer with the best ranking quality produced a negative downstream gain**: `pooled` reached a 3.83x lift on `mixed` — the highest in any experiment here — yet landed 0.0082 below random removal downstream. The budget went to the harmless types: duplicate and garbled together took ~365 of 1482 slots with near-zero harm, while genuinely harmful template got ~34. **Cleaning pays off only when all three hold: the noise is genuinely harmful, the scorer points the right way, and the budget lands on the harmful portion.** The third is specific to mixed streams and only visible once the chain reaches downstream evaluation (Section 6.13.3).

12. **Detectors are narrow, not broken, and unknown noise needs both legs pooled**: taking a single-type detector to a mixed noise stream drops overall AUC to 0.563-0.730, but restricting to "own type vs. clean" gives retention of 0.96-1.23 with no meaningful degradation — a coverage gap, not a population-shift failure. On genuinely unseen types (leave-one-out), the training-dynamics detector pool spans AUC 0.416-0.935 and `text_nn_sim` spans 0.396-0.974; they are complementary and each has types below random, so only pooling and taking the better score puts all 7 types above 0.66 (Section 6.12).

### 7.2 Limitations

- The overall spread in downstream benchmark impact is small (0.42-0.44 range), which could mean the current evaluation suite isn't sensitive enough, or that at this LoRA fine-tuning scale/epoch count, noise's downstream impact is genuinely limited; larger-scale or longer training could plausibly amplify these differences.
- Closed-loop cleaning now covers `garbled`, `template` and `mixed` (`pooled`), but has not been extended to the harder-to-detect keyword and near-duplicate types. **Every closed-loop conclusion rests on a single training run at a single seed, with no variance estimate**: the +0.0096 on template and the −0.0082 on mixed both sit inside the narrow 0.42-0.44 band of the 7-benchmark mean, so what is reliable is the direction (right direction > no cleaning > random > wrong direction) and the magnitude, not the fourth decimal.
- Section 6.11.4's minimal feature set uses **the same labels it is scored on** for greedy selection, so the AUC at a given k is optimistic: it measures how much signal a small set can carry, not a label-free recipe for choosing one. Section 3.1 forbids picking features by label, so "3 metrics beat 20" still needs a label-free selection mechanism before it can be cashed in production.
- The finding that `text_nn_sim` dominates duplicate/unrelated detection means the core "training-dynamics detection" methodology currently has substantive support only for types like garbled, template, truncation, and keyword — this should be stated carefully to avoid over-generalizing the claim.
- The "unseen noise types" in Section 6.12 are still among the 7 we injected ourselves, merely unseen by the detector pool; genuinely wild noise (mis-pasted content, encoding errors, cross-language contamination, machine-translation artifacts) may differ from these 7 in both text distribution and training dynamics, so the 0.416-0.935 range from leave-one-out cannot be extrapolated to wild data directly.
- The ablation in Sections 6.10 and 6.11 confirms that `cleaning_loop.py`'s current "20-feature mixed iforest" approach for duplicate/unrelated is being diluted by weak features other than `text_nn_sim` — scoring on `text_nn_sim` alone is more precise. near_duplicate/keyword, by contrast, are a genuine methodological blind spot with the current raw data categories, not a case of picking the wrong scoring method. Section 6.11.3's single-feature leave-one-out runs on the diagnostic-subsample population (~900-1200 rows per dataset), which is small enough that individual cells with Δ on the order of 0.01-0.02 should not be read one by one; what is reliable is the aggregate contrast — RF stays below 0.01 everywhere while IF exceeds it in 43 cells.

### 7.3 Next Steps

- Extend closed-loop cleaning to the remaining noise types, in particular checking whether cleaning the duplicate type (whose P@10% lift is < 1) is actually harmful rather than helpful.
- Repeat the template closed loop (Section 6.13.2) across multiple seeds to attach a variance estimate to the +0.0096 mean gain and the +0.0629 GSM8K recovery — both currently rest on a single training run.
- Add a `text_sim` scoring option to `cleaning_loop.py` for duplicate/unrelated (Section 6.11.4) — expected to be more precise than the current mixed-feature iforest, and needs no new data collection since `text_nn_sim` is already a full-coverage feature. Section 6.12.5's `pooled` already includes `text_nn_sim` as a leg and confirms its effect on duplicate (per-type AUC 0.946); what remains is a pure `text_sim` option, with no other legs mixed in, for calibrated single-type use.
- Explore dedicated features for "light perturbation" noise types like keyword and near-duplicate, for which the current combination of training-dynamics and text-similarity features carries very weak signal (Section 6.11 confirms this holds even after adding production-unavailable token diagnostics).
- **Harm-weighted removal rather than anomaly-weighted removal** (the most substantive open problem): Section 6.13.3 shows `pooled` spending about a quarter of its budget on duplicate/garbled, whose downstream harm is near zero, while genuinely harmful template receives 2.3%. Folding "how harmful is this kind of noise" into the score requires knowing the harm, which takes downstream evaluation — unavailable in a label-free setting. That circularity is the core difficulty this report leaves open.
- **Narrow the `iforest` leg from 20 features to 2-3** (Section 6.11.4): the only change that buys 0.04-0.34 with no new data and no new method, just fewer features. The obstacle is the same — *which* few depends on the noise type, so label-free feature selection has to be solved first.
- **Natural-noise closed loop (in progress)**: an OASST2 dataset is built (68,762 samples, human `quality` rating < 0.30 labelled as noise, 8.8% noise rate, see `wild_data.py`), with three training runs launched 2026-09-16. This is the missing step before extrapolating leave-one-out results to production. Two framing caveats must be reported alongside any result: the label is a human quality judgement rather than ground truth about a known perturbation (low quality conflates too-short, off-topic and disfluent, so it cannot be split by mechanism), and response length correlates with the noise label at −0.20 (noise median 101 characters vs. 674 overall), a signal fully visible to the detector that must be discounted from any detection result.
